There are several different note-taking styles, and each works better for different situations, whether you're studying, attending lectures, organizing projects, or preparing for exams.
Here are 8 popular styles, with examples of what they look like.
1. Cornell Notes
Como Fazer Anotações Eficientes em Reuniões: Guia Rápido
 
 
Divides your page into three sections: main notes, questions or keywords, and a summary.
Best for: College lectures, studying, and exam preparation.
Advantage: Makes reviewing and testing yourself easier.
2. Outline Method
Six Top Note Taking Strategies for College Students - Exam Study Expert
 
 
Organizes information using headings, subheadings, and indented bullet points.
Best for: Structured lectures, textbook chapters, and meeting notes.
Advantage: Easy to read, organize, and search.
3. Mind Mapping
Tips Membuat Nota Ringkas & Efektif: Teknik Belajar Paling Power!
 
 
Starts with a central topic and branches into related ideas.
Best for: Brainstorming, planning projects, and understanding relationships between concepts.
Advantage: Helps visual learners connect ideas.
4. Charting Method
Charting Method - Ms. Liew's Class
 
 
Uses tables with columns and rows to compare information.
Best for: Comparing theories, historical events, definitions, and research studies.
Advantage: Makes differences and similarities easy to identify.
5. Boxing Method
Six Top Note Taking Strategies for College Students - Exam Study Expert
 
 
Groups related information into separate visual boxes.
Best for: Digital notes, tablets, and reviewing multiple topics.
Advantage: Keeps information visually organized.
6. Sentence Method
11 Note-Taking Strategies That Help Students Learn
 
 
Records each new idea as a separate sentence or numbered statement.
Best for: Fast-paced lectures and meetings.
Advantage: Quick to write without worrying about formatting.
7. Flow Notes
Step 2: Preparing #Sketchnotes for Format, Flow, and Materials
 
 
Uses arrows, diagrams, short explanations, and connections to show how ideas develop.
Best for: Processes, programming concepts, and cause-and-effect relationships.
Advantage: Focuses on understanding rather than copying everything.
8. Zettelkasten Method
